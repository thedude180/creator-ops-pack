Lease-Operator Settlement Audit Kit

Your carrier writes the settlement. This is how you check it. Every week your settlement shows up in the carrier's format, with the carrier's labels — and a column of deductions you're expected to take on faith. Federal leasing rules are built on a simple test: your signed lease is the document that says what can be charged back to you (49 CFR 376.12 — and §376.12(h) makes the carrier spell out your escrow terms and account for the money). The problem was never the rule. The problem is nobody hands the driver the worksheet. This kit is the worksheet:

  • Settlement Audit Workbook (Sheets/Excel + CSVs) — log each week in minutes; the workbook computes your true per-mile rate and 4-week rolling average, checks every deduction line against your lease (section, allowed amount, variance), tracks your escrow against its cap, and reconciles fuel against your own receipts. A line that isn't in your lease — or an amount that doesn't match the lease math — flags itself.
  • Lease Deduction Checklist — the standard lines, what your lease must say for each, and the camouflage patterns Overdrive's "Numbers Game" investigation documented in carrier settlements.
  • First-Settlement Walkthrough — the 15-minute audit worked end-to-end on a sample settlement (sample numbers, clearly labeled), including a real-shaped catch: an escrow billed on the wrong miles.
  • Dispute / Question Kit — five fill-in templates for asking about a line in writing: lease-basis request, itemization request, escrow accounting, follow-up, escalation. Factual, professional, no legal threats.
  • Quick-Start Card — the whole weekly routine on one printed page.

Built by a working lease-operator (Stevens Trucking, KWS lease) who runs this audit on his own settlements — because the first settlement he ever got, he couldn't check either.
